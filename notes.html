Notes on the numbers

How each figure at the top of the main page was measured, and what it can and cannot show. All four come from the project's evaluation on generated practice attempts; none is yet measured on real children's recordings.

No notes lost8 of 8

What it measures

How many of a student's notes reach the scoring step when the student plays slower than the teacher.

How it was measured

An eight-note exercise was played back at controlled speeds and scored before and after a fix to how phrases are matched in time. The first version kept the phrase windows fixed to the teacher's timing, so a slower student's last notes fell outside and were dropped before scoring.

  • Real recordings add transcription noise, which this test does not include.

What it measures

How often a model's draft criticized something the scores did not measure at that student's level: usually tempo or timing for a beginner, where speed is deliberately not scored.

How it was measured

Three small models each wrote feedback for all 186 generated practice attempts, 558 drafts in total. Automatic checks flagged every draft that criticized an unscored dimension, before and after the models were fine-tuned.

Kind, as a child hears it32 of 33

What it measures

Whether the deployed model's feedback reads as warm and clear to a child.

How it was measured

A stronger AI model judged a shuffled, blinded sample of 135 drafts without knowing which model wrote each one. Of these, 33 came from the deployed model. Each draft was rated on five questions, one of which was "Would a child hear this as encouraging and clear?", on this scale:

3Warm, plain and encouraging throughout.
2Friendly, with a stiff phrase or two.
1Flat or clinical; technically fine but discouraging.
0Harsh, or full of jargon a beginner would not follow.

32 of the 33 drafts received a 3, and one received a 2, an average of 2.97 out of 3.

Why kindness alone is not enough

The sample also contained deliberate decoys: warm, well-written feedback attached to the wrong performance. They scored a perfect 3 on tone but only 0.50 out of 3 on accuracy. Kind words are easy; kind words that are also true are what the training achieved. The deployed model scored 2.67 out of 3 on accuracy, against 2.88 for the reference texts.

Limits

  • One judging round, by one AI judge, on 33 drafts.
  • The drafts mix practice attempts the model was trained on with ones it had not seen.
  • Tone was judged by a model, not by children or parents. Asking them is a planned next step.

Private by design0

What it means

No outside service is used to transcribe, score or write feedback. The transcription model, the grader and the language models all run within the teacher's account, and children's recordings, scores and feedback stay on the teacher's account.

One exception, during research only

To evaluate the models, a sample of generated practice feedback was judged by a stronger AI model in a separate chat. That sample came from simulated students only. No child's recording or feedback was part of it.
