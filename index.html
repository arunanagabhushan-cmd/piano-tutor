From notes to kind words.

A Tiered‑Scoring Pipeline and Teacher‑tailored Language Models to provide encouraging feedback to students based on their piano practice

By Aruna Nagabhushan, Neuqua Valley High School, Naperville, IL
Summer Internship 2026, Vanderbilt University
Under the guidance of Dr. Aniruddha Gokhale, Dr. Himanshu Neema and Dr. Yogesh Barve, Vanderbilt University

Children practice piano alone between lessons, and a mistake practiced all week gets learned. Piano Tutor scores each practice recording against the teacher's own recording, phrase by phrase. Small language models running within the teacher's account then turn the scores into short, encouraging feedback, and the teacher approves every word before the child sees it.

How it works

One recording, six steps, one human decision

The student never sees a raw score or an unreviewed draft. Each teacher correction is kept and becomes training data, so the drafts move toward that teacher's own voice.

  1. 1Record

    The student records an attempt in the browser, or uploads a file.

  2. 2Transcribe

    A piano transcription model turns audio into notes: pitch, onset, length, loudness.

  3. 3Clean

    Overtones, blips and fragments are removed from both the student's and the teacher's notes.

  4. 4Score by phrase

    Each phrase is compared with the teacher's recording on the dimensions the tier switches on.

  5. 5Draft

    Several local language models each write one short, kind sentence per phrase.

  6. 6Teacher approves

    The teacher picks a draft, edits it or writes their own. The edit becomes training data.

How it's built

One account, no outside services

Everything runs within the teacher's account: the web app, the scoring pipeline, the language models and the data. Recordings, scores and feedback never leave it.

The web appTeacher and student pages, sign-in, and a role check on every page.
Background workEach upload is graded by the scoring pipeline, then every active model drafts feedback in parallel.
Local modelsA local model server hosts the original models and the fine-tuned one.
DataRecords in MySQL; scores, drafts, corrections and the model registry in MongoDB; audio in file storage.
The harnessAn offline tool that reuses the same grader, tests it, and trains and registers tuned models.
DeploymentDocker Compose, with separate production and staging copies and backups.
Part I

A scoring pipeline you can trust

Feedback for a child is only as good as the numbers behind it. These are the scores the pipeline produces, the faults we found and fixed along the way, and how the final version was checked.

Strictness that grows with the student

Four tiers, each adding one thing to listen for

The teacher picks a tier for each assignment. There is no single overall score, because one number hides which part went wrong. Dimensions above the tier are left out of the data entirely, so nothing downstream can comment on them.

The beginner rule. At tiers 1 and 2, tempo is not scored. A beginner working slowly through a piece is doing exactly what they should.

How we made the scores trustworthy

Four faults, found and fixed

Each fault came to light by playing in performances whose correct score we already knew. Each fix is measured, before and after.

Slow students lost notes before scoring

Phrase windows fixed to the teacher's timing let a slower student's last notes fall outside, where they were silently dropped. The student was then told they had skipped half the piece. Windows now follow the student's own tempo, and every note is assigned to exactly one phrase.

Tempo

A performance at one-third speed scored perfectly

Timing measured evenness, so steady but very slow playing looked flawless. A separate tempo score and slow/fast flag were added, at tier 3 and above only, so beginners are still never penalized for going slowly.

The note-length score turned upside down

A student at half speed rightly holds notes twice as long, but raw lengths punished that. Below half speed the score inverted. Dividing by the fitted tempo makes it measure articulation, not speed.

Verifying the pipeline

Break it on purpose, and write down how

An evaluation harness takes a correct performance, injects known mistakes and records each one in a ledger. The pipeline's real grader then scores the result, so the right answer is known before anything runs.

  • Only mistakes that survive. If the pipeline's own clean-up would erase an injected mistake, it is drawn again, because a ledger must describe what the student actually played.
  • A simulated class. Six students with different learning rates work through 40 pieces, retrying and moving up tiers.
  • An independent check. Expected scores are computed from the ledger alone, without the grader's code.

Part II

Language models that encourage

With trustworthy scores in hand, small local models turn them into words. The models have to be kind and they have to be right. We evaluated them on both, then trained them.

Writing for a child

The rules every draft follows

Each rule was added after we saw drafts break it.

One sentence per phraseLonger drafts padded; a child reads one thing to work on.
Under 60 wordsShort enough to read at the piano.
No numbers, no jargon"Your pitch F1 was 0.45" means nothing to a seven-year-old.
Warm and plainEncouraging first, then one clear thing to try next.
Only what was measuredIf there is no tempo flag, the speed was fine. Never invent a speed problem.
Vary the wordingChange the phrasing between attempts, never the facts.
What prompting couldn't fix

Warm, but making things up

The scores show a few missed notes, perfectly even timing, and no tempo flag. All three off-the-shelf models still commented on speed or timing:

llama"The second section … is slightly slower than expected."
qwen"In the second section, you rushed a bit."
gemma"…could be smoother in terms of timing."

Across 186 cases the base models did this 34–58% of the time at beginner tiers where tempo isn't scored at all. Rewording the prompt didn't change it. When a real tempo flag was present they reported it 17 times out of 18. The problem is volunteering advice nobody measured.

Evaluating the feedback

Five criteria, one of them about the child

Every draft is scored 0–3 on factual accuracy, tier awareness, naming the right phrase, length, and tone. The tone criterion asks one question:

Would a child hear this as encouraging and clear?

Two instruments do the scoring. Fast pattern checks catch jargon, harsh words and claims about unmeasured things on every draft. A stronger model judges a blinded sample. The sample includes control rows: kind, fluent feedback deliberately attached to the wrong performance.

Training the models

Fine-tuning fixed what prompting couldn't

Each of the 186 harness attempts got a reference message written from its ledger, in a warm teacher's voice and limited to what was measured. Three small models were fine-tuned on these with QLoRA on one 8 GB consumer GPU. Off-the-shelf tone scores had ranged from 1.78 to 2.17; tuned, all three reached 2.88 or higher. The tuned gemma 2B, the smallest, was ranked first by the blind judge and is now deployed.

What the measuring taught us

Lessons that apply beyond music

Saying nothing can win

A model whose conversion had silently broken wrote one word per case and won three of four headline metrics. Metrics that count mistakes avoided need a length floor.

1 word per case · 3 of 4 metrics won

Teacher edit distance measures length

Ranking models by how much the teacher retyped tracks how long the draft was, not how good. The ranking screen now shows length beside it.

ρ ≈ 0 with quality · strong with length

Compare at the same settings

A fine-tuning gain of +2.00 fell to +0.54 once the base model ran at the same temperature. Tuned and base models react to decoding settings differently.

+2.00 → +0.54

Two judges beat one

Wherever the quick checks and the stronger judge disagreed, the judge was right, and each disagreement pointed to a fault in the checks.

How it compares

Where Piano Tutor fits among practice apps

Consumer apps such as Yousician, Simply Piano and Skoove are excellent at instant note-by-note correction. They tell a student that a note was wrong, not what to practice, and the teacher isn't part of them. Teacher platforms such as SmartMusic, Piano Marvel and MatchMySound bring the teacher back, but what they generate automatically is a score. Piano Tutor works in the space between: it drafts the words, and the teacher approves them.

Beyond piano

Built with piano, ready for other teachers

The tiered scores, the child-centered rules, teacher approval, the tailoring loop and the harness don't depend on the instrument. A new subject needs its own transcriber, its own tiers and its own typical mistakes.

Indian classical violin

Notes become a continuous pitch contour relative to the performer's tonic, because ornaments (gamakas) glide around pitches. Possible tiers:

  1. Correct svaras
  2. Phrase order
  3. Gamaka shape and intonation
  4. Rhythm within the tāla, tempo and bowing

Guided Sanskrit reading

Syllables (akṣara) are aligned against the known text, which serves as the reference. Possible tiers:

  1. Correct syllables
  2. Order and omissions
  3. Vowel length (hrasva / dīrgha), the counterpart of piano's note length
  4. Pace, and pitch accents for Vedic recitation
